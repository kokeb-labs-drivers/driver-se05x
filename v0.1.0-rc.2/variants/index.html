Where to use it: which variant¶

The family has three chips, many configurations and two applet generations. This page helps you choose where each fits and which one to order; the per-chip guides (SE050, SE051, SE052) cover wiring and what differs.

Datasheet sections this page relies on
  • I2C clock and clock stretching per variant: NXP SE050-DATASHEET Rev. 3.8, 4.1.1 Supported I2C frequencies, p. 9
  • SE050 ordering and variants: NXP SE050-DATASHEET Rev. 3.8, Table 3 Ordering information, p. 10
  • SE051 ordering and variants: NXP SE051 Rev. 2.0, Table 2 Ordering information, p. 11
  • Feature bits of the extended version: NXP AN12543 Rev. 4.5, Table 9, p. 26
  • Limits of FIPS 140-3 mode: NXP AN12543 Rev. 4.5, 3.13.2 FIPS 140-3 mode, p. 45
  • GetVersion, GetTimestamp, GetFreeMemory, GetRandom: NXP AN12543 Rev. 4.5, Generic management commands, p. 151
  • SE050 variant identifiers: NXP AN12436 Rev. 2.4, Table 2 Variant identifiers, p. 8
  • SE051 OEF identifiers per variant: NXP AN12973 Rev. 2.0, Table 4, p. 4
  • SE052F configuration: applet 7.2.22, AppletConfig 0x26F2, OEF B501: NXP AN14277 Rev. 1.0, SE052F configuration, p. 2
  • GetFreeMemory reply width: NXP AN13030 Rev. 2.7, GetFreeMemory, p. 641

All of them, with the driver API for each: Datasheet map.

Figure description and sources

Read top to bottom; take the first question you answer yes. Certification needs come first: FIPS 140-3 Level 3 is the SE052F; FIPS 140-2 with SCP03 mandatory is the SE050F. Then interfaces: NFC commissioning and Matter (PAKE) is the SE051H, UWB the SE051W. Field update of the applet (SEMS Lite) is the SE051A or C. Otherwise the SE050 by algorithm set: E for AES-GCM and CCM and Curve448 (applet 7.x), C for ECC and RSA, A for ECC only, B for RSA only, D for ECC with MIFARE DESFire. The SE051P runs a customer applet and has no IoT applet, so this driver does not apply. The bottom row is the authentication choice: platform SCP03 whenever the bus can be probed, an ECKey session to bind a host, a UserID session only under a trusted host OS, since it has no secure messaging. The driver reads the real capabilities at Open() and never trusts the part name.

Where each fits¶

Your product Choose Why
A connected device that needs a device identity, TLS client keys and secret storage, no certification SE050E or SE050C The IoT applet with ECC (E adds AES-GCM and CCM, applet 7.2); smallest package
A product that must use FIPS 140-2 validated key storage SE050F FIPS 140-2 certified chip; platform SCP03 always on (SE050 guide)
A product that must use FIPS 140-3 Level 3 validated key storage SE052F FIPS 140-3 certified chip; mind the APDU throughput limit (AN13904 p. 28)

A fleet whose applet must be updatable in the field SE051A or SE051C SEMS Lite applet update (SE051 data sheet Table 2, p. 11)

The chips¶

The SE050 and SE051 share the HX2QFN20 package and its pinout; the SE052 comes in the larger HVQFN20 with a different pinout and no ENA pin (SE052 data sheet section 6, p. 5, and Table 2, p. 4).

SE052 (HVQFN20)¶

Figure description and sources

The SE052 seen from the top in its HVQFN20 package (4 x 4 x 0.85 mm, 0.5 mm pitch, SOT917-7 in the data sheet). Pin 1 is at the top of the left side and the numbers run counter-clockwise: 1 to 5 down the left side, 6 to 10 along the bottom, 11 to 15 up the right side and 16 to 20 along the top. Each pin card is coloured by the pin's type as in the HX2QFN20 pinout. The driver uses SDA (11) and SCL (10) for the bus and RST_N (14) through the reset port; the SE052 has no ENA pin, so deep power-down is a T=1 S-block (0xDF) and the next I2C access wakes it. The table is SE052 Table 2 (p. 4).

Drawn from: docs/diagrams/figures/_se052.py; figure module docs/diagrams/figures/se052-pinout.py.

Figure description and sources

The SE052 body from above and from the side, to one scale: 4.0 x 4.0 mm, 0.85 mm high, 20 pins at a 0.5 mm pitch, five on each side, pin 1 at the top of the left side (SE052 data sheet section 6, p. 5, and the pin order of Table 2, p. 4). The terminal and exposed-pad dimensions are in the package drawing SOT917-7, not in the data sheet, so the pins are drawn as position marks only. The SE050 and SE051 come in the smaller HX2QFN20 (3.0 x 3.0 x 0.32 mm, 0.4 mm pitch).

Drawn from: docs/diagrams/figures/_se052.py; figure module docs/diagrams/figures/se052-outline.py.

Figure description and sources

The SE052 on an MCU's I2C bus. SDA (pin 11) and SCL (pin 10) go to the MCU with pull-ups to the bus supply of 1.65 V to 3.6 V, which also feeds VIN (pin 12). VOUT (pin 15) goes to VDD (pin 18) on the board, as Table 2 asks; Figure 1 shows an optional 22 nF on VIN and an optional 22 nF (or nothing) on VOUT. RST_N (pin 14) goes to a GPIO the reset port drives low to reset the chip. There is no ENA pin: deep power-down is the S-block 0xDF, and the next I2C access wakes the chip. CLK (13) is left open or tied to ground, LA and LB are left open, IO1 and IO2 are the chip's own I2C controller and stay open when it is not used. The pull-up value is not in the data sheet.

What differs by applet¶

  • 3.x (SE050 A to D, F): attestation format 1 (nonce echo), ECDAA, no AES-GCM or CCM, 2-byte free-memory reply. The HRoT facade (hrot::Device) does not run here: every facade call returns Unsupported before anything is sent, after its argument checks (an invalid key, an off-curve point or an empty buffer is refused first with InvalidArgument). Use se05x::Device directly on these chips.
  • 6.x (SE051 A2 and C2 on early boards, OEF A565 and A564): no specification in hand; the policy builder uses the 3.x rule table. The HRoT facade does not run here either: every facade call returns Unsupported before anything is sent, after the same argument checks. Use se05x::Device, or update the applet to 7.x.
  • 7.x (SE050E, SE051, SE052): the HRoT facade runs; attestation format 2 (the command is signed), AES-GCM, CCM and GMAC, Curve448 on SE050E and SE051, PAKE on parts with the feature bits, ReadState, TriggerSelfTest, DisableObjectCreation; the extended version carries 30 feature bytes (AN12543 Table 9).
  • FIPS modes: the SE050F refuses RSA 1024, plain RSA, 2-key 3DES, SHA-1 signatures and curves under 224 bits; the SE052F has no EdDSA, Montgomery curves, DES, MIFARE DESFire or PAKE (AN12543 3.13.2 FIPS 140-3 mode, p. 45).

The driver reads the chip, not the name¶

Open() reads the applet version and AppletConfig, the extended feature bits on 7.x, the OEF id and the free-memory width, and fills Capabilities. Every command passes the capability gate before it is sent: a command the chip does not have is Unsupported and costs no round trip. The OEF ids per configuration are in AN12436 Table 2 (SE050) and AN12973 Table 4 (SE051); where two tables of one note disagree, the chip's own value wins.

Not for this driver¶

The SE051P runs a customer applet and has no IoT applet. The T4T applet of the SE051H and the SEMS Lite update agent are outside v1 (Limitations).
